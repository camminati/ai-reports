How far to trust these numbers

Definitions differ. “Use”, “pilot”, “production” and “failure” mean different things in each source.

Self-reported. Most figures are survey answers, not audited results. Bitkom says so itself.

Interested parties. Gartner, McKinsey and BCG sell advice, and their methods are only partly public.

Thin evidence. MIT NANDA’s widely quoted “95% of organizations see no measurable return” rests on an anonymized, self-reported sample; treat it as a signal only.

Snapshots. Eurostat and Bitkom are cross-sections, so drop-out cannot be measured directly.

Gaps. No verified EU industry split and no public data on agentic-project cancellations.

Next official data: Eurostat update planned for December 2026.

Reading guide. Definitions: McKinsey and Stanford count regular use in at least one function; Eurostat counts any of a list of AI technologies; Census BTOS changed its question in Nov 2025; Bitkom is self-assessed. Consultancy and analyst sources: Gartner, McKinsey and BCG are commercial research houses. McKinsey 2026 is an online survey of 1,719 people in 97 countries, weighted by country GDP, with 36% from organizations above $1bn revenue; its shares use different bases (all respondents, AI users, or high performers, n = 92), so they are not one strict funnel. MIT NANDA (Jul 2025): the widely quoted 95% of organizations seeing no measurable return comes from a preliminary report; sample anonymized, outcomes self-reported. Cross-sections: Eurostat reports usage in a reference year, not the same firms over time, so a de-adoption rate cannot be identified from it. Gaps: we did not find a verified EU or German industry breakdown of AI use, nor public data on agentic-project cancellations. Next official data: Eurostat plans an update in December 2026. Some aggregator pages quote Destatis figures for Germany that we could not verify.
