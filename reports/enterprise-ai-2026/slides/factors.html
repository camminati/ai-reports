McKinsey State of AI 2026: high performers (5% or more of EBIT attributable to AI, plus "significant" value; about 6% of respondents) are more than three times as likely to intend transformative change and nearly three times as likely to have fundamentally redesigned individual workflows. The 2026 report PDF contains no relative-weights ranking; an earlier reading of McKinsey's web article cited 31 variables, which we could not confirm in the PDF. What the PDF does show: nearly three-quarters of high performers (up from 55% last year) have fundamentally redesigned workflows because of AI, against one-quarter of other respondents (n = 92 vs 1,429), and in Exhibit 11 this is the widest gap of the eleven practices shown (our reading of the chart). High performers are also 3.3 times as likely to intend to transform their business within three years, twice as likely to say senior leaders demonstrate commitment and that impact-measurement processes exist, and more than three times as likely to be scaling agents in most functions. Size: McKinsey (organizations above $1bn scaling agents 40%, up from 27%; smaller flat at 22%) and Eurostat (large 55.03%, small 17%). Buy versus build: the MIT NANDA July 2025 report is widely quoted as saying vendor partnerships succeed about twice as often as internal builds (about 67% vs 33%); we saw this only in second-hand summaries, and the report rests on an anonymized, self-reported sample, so treat it as a weak signal. Survey-based associations show what differs between groups; they do not prove that redesign causes the profit impact.
