AI leaders’ forecasts

Why leaders say what they say: context, critics, and a measurement

Documented context

3 Mar 2025: Anthropic raises $3.5bn at $61.5bn. 10 Mar: Amodei’s “90% of code” talk.

22 May 2025: Claude 4 launches. 28 May: Amodei’s jobs warning; motive stated: “a duty and obligation to be honest”.

Aug 2025: Altman says investors are “overexcited” while OpenAI raises at $300bn.

28 Oct 2025: research-intern goal announced on the day OpenAI completed its for-profit recapitalisation.

Critics and walk-backs

White House AI adviser Sacks, Oct 2025: Anthropic runs “a sophisticated regulatory capture strategy based on fear-mongering”. Amodei replied publicly.

Kristian Kersting (TU Darmstadt), Mar 2025: AGI claims can be a business strategy for AI firms.

Fortune, May 2026: Altman “delighted to be wrong” on jobs; Amodei: if 90% of a job is automated, people do the other 10%.

Karpathy, Oct 2025: “a decade of agents”, not a year.

Measured, not predicted: METR’s Jul 2025 trial found experienced developers 19% slower with AI tools, while expecting to be about a quarter faster (16 developers, 246 tasks; a snapshot of early-2025 tools). METR’s Feb 2026 follow-up called its new data an “unreliable signal”.

Our reading (interpretation): timing beside a fundraising round or launch does not prove motive, and no source states these forecasts were made to raise money. But every forecaster on the previous slide sells or invests in the technology, so treat their timelines as claims to test.

Sources: Anthropic [66]; Axios [61]; TechCrunch [69]; Fortune [67, 68]; METR [70].
