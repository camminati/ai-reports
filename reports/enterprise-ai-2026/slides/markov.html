How to read it

Think of subscription churn: if 5% of users leave every year, the user base levels off below 100%.

The 5% is an assumption. US data show 14.5% of AI users expected to stop within six months (Bonney et al., as cited).

The sample starts with 68.9% already using AI, so the levels are illustrative. The logic is the finding.

Source: Hojdik et al. [52], J. Open Innovation 12(3), Sep 2026 (816 Slovak firms).

Hojdik, Stetka, Grisakova, Hajduova, Banda and Pallerova, "AI diffusion as open innovation dynamics: Forecasting adoption, de-adoption, and stationary equilibrium in Slovak enterprises", Journal of Open Innovation: Technology, Market, and Complexity 12(3), Sep 2026. Data: cross-sectional survey of 816 firms (562 adopters, 131 planners, 123 non-adopters; 68.87% adopters at t = 0). Model: annual Markov transitions from non-adopter to planner to adopter, with scenario-specific transition matrices (optimistic, baseline, conservative). With adoption absorbing, long-run outcomes approach near-saturation. With a 5% annual de-adoption rate, adoption stabilizes at about 87.80% (optimistic), 83.88% (baseline) and 72.85% (conservative). The 5% is an assumption anchored on a ZEW switch-back signal, which ZEW itself says may reflect measurement ambiguity. The authors note that cross-sectional official statistics cannot identify de-adoption, and call for longitudinal validation. This is the only model we found that covers both adoption and reversal; it does not model project failure directly.
