Six numbers to track in your own company

Use

Share of staff or units using AI each week

Public range: 18% to 89%, by definition.

Scale

Share of use cases running beyond one team

McKinsey: 44% of AI users say AI is scaling.

Value

Use cases with a measured profit or cost effect

McKinsey: 37% any EBIT impact, about 6% significant.

Conversion

Pilots that reach production

S&P: on average 46% of projects scrapped before wide use. Gartner: 50%+ abandoned.

Run cost

Monthly cost per completed AI workflow

Gartner: agentic inference cost up 5×+ through 2028.

Drop-out

Teams that stop using AI after launch

Model assumes 5% a year. US users: 14.5% expect to stop within six months.

Benchmarks: McKinsey [1], S&P Global [2], Gartner [5, 48], Hojdik et al. [52].

34

These six metrics mirror the funnel in the public data, so that a company can place itself against it: use, scale, value, conversion from pilot to production, run cost and drop-out. The context lines quote the benchmarks used earlier: the public range for use (Census BTOS 18% to McKinsey 89%); McKinsey 2026 scaling 44% (of AI users), any EBIT impact 37%, high performers about 6%; S&P Global VotE 2025 average of 46% of projects scrapped between proof of concept and broad adoption; Gartner Jan 2026 at least 50% of GenAI projects abandoned after proof of concept; Gartner Aug 2026 on inference cost; Hojdik et al. 5% annual de-adoption assumption and the Bonney et al. 14.5% six-month stop expectation (as cited there). The metric definitions are our suggestion, not a standard.
