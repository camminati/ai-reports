No validated model covers adoption and failure

S-curve diffusion (Bass)

Forecasts: How fast use spreads to a fixed pool of eventual users.

Evidence: No enterprise GenAI validation found in our sources.

Adoption only

Gartner Hype Cycle

Forecasts: Stages from hype through disillusionment to plateau.

Evidence: Steinert and Leifer found Gartner’s placement did not match data for three technologies.

A narrative, not a forecast

Markov chain with drop-out

Forecasts: Firms moving between non-user, planner, user and back out.

Evidence: Fitted to 816 Slovak firms at one point in time (Hojdik et al., 2026).

Best structure, untested

Stated plans vs outcomes

Forecasts: Firms’ own six-month plans, compared with later use.

Evidence: The only real back-test we found; it held (Census BTOS).

Short-term only

Sources: Hojdik et al. [52]; Steinert and Leifer [51]; Federal Reserve [8].

What we looked for: a model that jointly forecasts adoption and failure over time and has been validated. We did not find one. Bass and logistic S-curves are standard diffusion tools; they assume adopters stay adopters. Gartner Hype Cycle: Steinert and Leifer (and the ScienceDirect review "The hype cycle model: A review and future directions") compared Gartner's reported progression for tidal power, IGCC and photovoltaic generation with Google News and Google Insight data and found incongruences. Hojdik et al. (Journal of Open Innovation, Sep 2026) build a discrete-time Markov chain with states non-adopter, planner, adopter, calibrated on 816 Slovak enterprises via a cross-sectional survey; they state that a longitudinal validation is still needed. The Census BTOS asks firms about planned use over the next six months, which allows expectation versus realization checks. Our reading of each model is a judgement, not a published rating.
