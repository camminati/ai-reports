Agent forecasts: what early indicators show

Forecast

Gartner, Jun 2025: over 40% of agentic AI projects canceled by end-2027

Consistent so far, but indirect

  • Related count for GenAI: at least 50% abandoned after proof of concept by end-2025, against 30% forecast (Gartner, Jan 2026).
  • Firms scrapping most AI initiatives before production: 17% to 42% in one year (S&P Global, 2025).
  • Agent scaling is uneven: 40% of $1bn+ firms, up from 27%; smaller firms flat at 22% (McKinsey, 2026).

Forecast

Gartner, May 2026: 40% of enterprises demote or scrap AI agents by 2027 over governance gaps

Incidents are common; rollbacks are not measured

  • 53% of organizations had an AI agent exceed its intended permissions (Cloud Security Alliance, n = 445, Sep–Nov 2025).
  • 47% had a security incident involving an AI agent in the past year (same survey, commissioned by Zenity).
  • Only 31% had formally adopted agent governance policies (same survey).

Indicators measure related things, not the forecast itself. They show direction, not proof; no source counts agent cancellations directly.

Sources: Gartner [5, 42, 43]; S&P Global [2]; McKinsey [1]; Cloud Security Alliance [44].

21

Early indicators for the two agent-related Gartner forecasts. (1) Agentic cancellations (Gartner Jun 2025, over 40% by end-2027): no agent-specific cancellation count is public. Closest measures: Gartner Jan 2026 says at least 50% of GenAI projects were abandoned after proof of concept by end-2025 (its own 2024 forecast was 30%); S&P Global VotE 2025 says the share of companies abandoning most AI initiatives before production rose from 17% to 42%; McKinsey 2026 says 40% of respondents from organizations above $1bn revenue are scaling agents (27% last year) while smaller ones stayed flat at 22%. These cover GenAI or AI in general, not agents. (2) Governance (Gartner May 2026, 40% of enterprises demote or decommission agents by 2027 after production incidents): the Gartner release contains no survey data. The Cloud Security Alliance study (445 IT and security professionals, Sep and Nov 2025, commissioned by Zenity) found 53% saw agents exceed intended permissions, 47% had a security incident involving an agent in the past year, 31% had formally adopted governance policies. The study has no data on rollbacks, so it shows the preconditions, not the outcome. Vendor-commissioned survey; treat with care.
