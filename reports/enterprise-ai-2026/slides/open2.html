Data and cost forecasts: what early indicators show

Forecast

Gartner, Feb 2025: 60% of AI projects without AI-ready data abandoned through 2026

The gap is visible in every survey

  • 63% of organizations lack, or are unsure they have, the right data practices for AI (Gartner, n = 1,203, Jul 2024).
  • Only 7% say their data is completely ready for AI; 27% say not very or not at all ready (Cloudera and HBR, n > 230, Oct 2025).
  • Only 5% say their data is ready to support AI initiatives (Dun and Bradstreet, n = 10,000, May 2026).

Forecast

Gartner, Aug 2026: inference cost per agentic workflow up more than fivefold through 2028

Unit price falls, workflow cost rises

  • Price per token is falling: 280-fold drop for GPT-3.5-level quality, Nov 2022 to Oct 2024 (Stanford AI Index 2025).
  • A task routed to an agentic reasoning model costs at least 5× a chatbot answer, often much more (Gartner, Aug 2026).
  • One in five firms limit AI use because of operating costs, tokens included (McKinsey, 2026).

Surveys differ in sample and wording, so the data figures are not one time series. The cost trend is a mechanism, not yet a measured bill.

Sources: Gartner [45, 48]; Cloudera and HBR [46]; CIO on Dun and Bradstreet [47]; Stanford HAI [49]; McKinsey [1].

Early indicators for the data and cost forecasts. (1) AI-ready data (Gartner Feb 2025: through 2026, organizations will abandon 60% of AI projects unsupported by AI-ready data). Gartner survey of 1,203 data management leaders, July 2024: 63% do not have or are unsure whether they have the right data management practices for AI. Cloudera and Harvard Business Review Analytic Services (press release 5 Mar 2026; more than 230 respondents, October 2025): 7% say their data is completely ready, 27% not very or not at all ready, 73% found data preparation challenging. Dun and Bradstreet AI Momentum Survey (10,000 businesses, reported by CIO on 13 May 2026): 5% say their data is ready to support AI initiatives; barriers are data access (50%), privacy and compliance (44%), quality (40%). The three surveys have different populations and definitions, so they show a persistent gap, not a trend line. The abandonment outcome itself has not been measured. (2) Inference cost (Gartner Aug 2026: cost per agentic workflow up more than fivefold through 2028). Gartner names an inference paradox: tokens get cheaper but more capable models and multi-step workflows consume far more of them. Stanford AI Index 2025: cost of a GPT-3.5-level query fell from $20 to $0.07 per million tokens between Nov 2022 and Oct 2024, more than 280-fold; McKinsey 2026: one in five respondents limit AI use because of operating costs including tokens. We saw a secondary claim of a tripling in enterprise LLM spend and 93% exceeding token budgets attributed to McKinsey; McKinsey's own page does not contain it, so we left it out. Colour key (our reading of the evidence): green = came as predicted; yellow = not yet, but on track; red = missed or off track; grey = cannot be checked. Both forecasts run to 2026–2028, so no result is final; yellow means the indicators point the predicted way.
