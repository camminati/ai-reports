Summary

Four findings that hold across the sources

Use is broad, value is narrow

About 89% of surveyed firms use AI somewhere, 37% report any profit impact, and about 6% report a significant one (McKinsey 2026).

Failure means stopped pilots

S&P Global: 42% of firms scrapped most initiatives (2025). Public cases (Klarna, Commonwealth Bank) reversed AI-led cuts. Gartner: at least 50% of GenAI projects abandoned after proof of concept.

No model forecasts adoption and failure together

Adoption curves ignore drop-out. The best available model includes drop-out but is untested over time.

Workflow redesign leads the factors

Data readiness, run-cost control and governance follow. Larger firms scale more often.

Sources: McKinsey State of AI 2026 [1]; S&P Global [2]; company statements [3, 4]; Gartner [5].

Four findings. (1) The funnel: 89% of surveyed organizations use AI in at least one function (McKinsey 2026, Exhibit 1; 88% a year earlier, the figure the Stanford AI Index 2026 reports); 44% of AI-using organizations say AI is scaling across the enterprise; 37% of respondents report at least some enterprise-level EBIT impact; about 6% qualify as high performers (5% or more of EBIT from AI and significant value). (2) Failure is mostly pilots that stop before production: Gartner Jan 2026 says at least 50% of GenAI projects were abandoned after proof of concept by end-2025; S&P Global 2025 says 42% of companies abandoned most initiatives (up from 17%). (3) Models: we found no validated model that forecasts adoption and failure jointly. (4) Factors: in McKinsey 2026, nearly three-quarters of high performers have fundamentally redesigned workflows against one-quarter of other respondents.
