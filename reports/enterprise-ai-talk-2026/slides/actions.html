[3 min] Five actions, one for each pattern. They are our synthesis of the evidence on the previous slides, not the finding of any one source. For a CFO: 1 and 5 are budget rules, 2 is a staffing rule, 3 is a risk rule and 4 is a contract rule. 1. Redesign the workflow, not only the tool. Fund the redesign, and not just the licences. This is the widest gap between high performers and the others (about 3 times). Ask of each use case: which steps disappear, who checks the result, and what changes in the process? 2. Prove quality before you cut roles. Run the AI beside the team first, measure quality and volumes, and only then decide about headcount. Klarna and Commonwealth Bank paid for doing it the other way round. 3. Treat every customer-facing bot as your own voice, and match governance to how autonomous each agent is. Name an owner. Air Canada was held liable for its chatbot, and Gartner warns that uniform governance across agents leads to failure; it expects 40% of enterprises to demote or scrap agents over governance gaps by 2027 (a forecast, not yet measured). 4. Plan the exit before you sign. Ask for data and model portability and for price-change terms, and keep a tested way out. 59% name lock-in as the main barrier to switching cloud provider, and VMware customers reported increases of 800 to 1,500%. 5. Start with a narrow, measured task. Before each pilot, agree kill-or-scale rules and a run-cost budget: Gartner expects the run cost of one agentic workflow to rise more than fivefold through 2028, and names escalating cost and unclear value as top causes of abandonment. How these relate to the report's own list on slide 35 of the full report: the report's five actions overlap (workflow, governance, kill-or-scale and run cost) and add drop-out tracking and benchmarking on "in production", which appear on the next slide.
