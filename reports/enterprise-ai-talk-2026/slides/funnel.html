[2 min] Finding 1: use is broad, value is narrow. Read the four numbers left to right as a funnel. McKinsey, The state of AI in 2026 (1,719 respondents in 97 countries, fielded May and June 2026, weighted by each country's share of global GDP). 89% say their organisation uses AI regularly in at least one function (88% a year earlier). 44% of the organisations that use AI say it is scaling across the enterprise (38% a year earlier); of all respondents that would be roughly 39%, our arithmetic. 37% report at least some enterprise-level EBIT impact, unchanged year on year. About 6% are what McKinsey calls high performers: 5% or more of EBIT from AI and significant value. The bases differ: all respondents, AI users, high performers. So read it as a picture, not as one strict funnel. BCG (Sep 2025, 1,250 companies) shows the same shape from a different survey: 22% beyond proof of concept, 60% hardly any material value despite substantial investment, 5% creating value at scale. All figures are self-reported by executives, and McKinsey and BCG sell advice. Say it once, here. Key sentence: nearly everyone has started; few can show the profit. Full report: slide 7.
