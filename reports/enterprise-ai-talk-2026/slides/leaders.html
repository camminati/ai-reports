[2 min] Finding 3, second half: forecasts by the people who build the technology. They are also interested parties: they sell AI or raise money for it. The traffic-light verdicts are our judgement from public evidence, not a published rating. 1. Amodei (Anthropic), Mar 2025: AI would write 90% of code within three to six months. Anthropic reported 70 to 90% of code company-wide in January 2026 and Google 75% of new code in April 2026. Both are self-measured, and there is no industry figure. Yellow: held where it is measured, unverified outside. 2. Amodei, May 2025: AI could wipe out half of entry-level white-collar jobs in one to five years and lift unemployment to 10 to 20%. US unemployment was 4.1% in August 2026. Stanford's Digital Economy Lab finds fewer hires of exposed 22 to 25-year-olds (19% below peers), but no economy-wide displacement. McKinsey: 14% of AI users report AI-linked headcount cuts. We mark it red because the evidence so far does not match, but the window runs to 2030, so it is not settled. 3. Altman (OpenAI), Jan 2025: first AI agents "may join the workforce" in 2025. Deloitte: 11% of organisations had agents in production. Karpathy (Feb 2026): coding agents "didn't work before December". Yellow: late, then real. 4. Zuckerberg (Meta), Jan to Apr 2025: an AI "mid-level engineer" in 2025 and half of development by AI within a year. We found no Meta figure; in July 2026 Meta said agents had not progressed as fast as hoped. Red. 5. Altman, Oct 2025: an automated AI research intern by September 2026. OpenAI says it reached that on its own definition. Grey, nobody outside can check. Takeaway for a decision maker: treat bold timelines as marketing until a neutral source confirms them.
