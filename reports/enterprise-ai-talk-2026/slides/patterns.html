[3 min] The most likely causes, as five patterns that link choices to outcomes. Read each row as "if a company does this, then the sources show this". The evidence column is our own assessment of how well each link is supported, not a published rating. 1. AI added to old tasks while the workflow stays the same. Broad use, rare profit: 44% scaling, 37% any EBIT impact, about 6% significant (McKinsey 2026; BCG shows the same shape). Strong, because large surveys agree, but self-reported. 2. People are cut before quality is proven. Klarna and Commonwealth Bank reversed cuts; McDonald's ended a pilot. Medium: public cases, no count of how often it happens. 3. A bot speaks for the company, and the company answers for it. Moffatt v Air Canada, 2024. Medium: one ruling, but a clear principle. 4. One vendor holds the data, models and licences. 59% of German companies name lock-in as the main barrier to switching cloud provider (Bitkom Cloud Report 2026), 64% saw cloud costs rise in 2025, and European customers reported VMware price increases of 800 to 1,500% after Broadcom's takeover (reported by CISPE, an industry association, so an interested party). Medium. 5. A narrow, measured task with a clean process. Documented gains: German insurers report claims handled in 10 minutes instead of weeks (DOMCURA), a UK government trial reported 26 minutes saved per day (self-reported). Weak: company-reported, selected cases. Say: patterns, not laws. They are correlations from surveys and cases. But they point in a consistent direction, and they lead directly to the actions.
